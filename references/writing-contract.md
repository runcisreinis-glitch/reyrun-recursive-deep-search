# Mandatory readable, scientific and friendly delivery

Apply the selected Humanizer method to every substantive answer, report, installation explanation, public example and promotional text. The method must actually be read and applied; a claimed tool name or a banned-word scan is not evidence of a rewrite.

Read the included [Humanizer 3.1.0 procedure](humanizer-upstream.md) in full and use its embedded/file mode. Its upstream is [blader/humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer); the original [MIT notice](HUMANIZER_LICENSE.txt) is retained. Check for a relevant update once per substantial writing batch and record the selection; do not silently replace the pinned copy mid-evaluation. The host model performs this editorial procedure. It is not a promise of a separate autonomous rewriting engine or an AI-detector score.

1. Preserve the original draft and an invariant list: facts, names, numbers, dates, sources, negations, conditions, uncertainty, technical meanings and scope.
2. Rewrite for a clear recommendation, connected explanation, concrete examples, useful alternatives and an understandable next step. Use a friendly, polite tone without condescension or assumptions about the user's intelligence. Do not use demographic insults as difficulty labels.
3. Provide depth proportional to the question. A difficult question deserves substantive treatment; a simple question does not need artificial complexity. Innovation must explain a useful mechanism or testable alternative.
4. Compare the entire revised text with the draft and evidence. Restore any lost caveat, changed number, unsupported fact or distorted source attribution.
5. Save draft and final SHA-256, method/version, actual editor identity and the semantic review result. A missing pass or unresolved factual change leaves the answer a draft.

Scientific language means precise claims with evidence and stated uncertainty, expressed in words the intended reader can follow. It does not mean jargon, impersonal prose or exaggerated authority.
